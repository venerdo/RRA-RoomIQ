# Backend Architecture Baseline

## Target architecture

The backend is designed around independent service ownership and a shared API entry pattern.

## Core principles

- Microservices boundaries are based on business ownership.
- Service-to-service communication happens through HTTP or asynchronous events.
- Scheduling business routes are guarded by service-side bearer validation and Identity authorization. Callers propagate an Identity-issued user token; the actor and permissions are resolved by Identity, not supplied by a caller. Scheduling has no API Gateway route in Stage 5; production deployments must keep the service endpoint and its OpenAPI surfaces on a private/trusted network.
- Scheduling owns calendar rules and availability calculations; confirmed reservation occupancy and its authoritative overlap barrier belong to Booking Service.
- The identity service is authoritative for auth, roles, permissions, scopes, and privilege evaluation.
- Database ownership is maintained per service.

## Current phase

Stage 2 identity requirements S2-01 through S2-18 and Stage 3 S3-01 through S3-11 are complete and accepted. Stage 4 S4-01 through S4-12 and Stage 5 S5-01 through S5-07 are complete and accepted. Room Service owns the nine ERD room/resource tables, Room APIs, Identity-delegated mutation authorization, PostgreSQL integrity, and the shared response/error/OpenAPI contract. Scheduling owns calendar/policy/availability calculations and constraints; bounded recurrence evaluation returns local dates, S5-05 validates candidate local times, and S5-06 returns continuous candidate intervals using Room owner APIs and a protected Booking snapshot contract. Candidate-producing searches fail closed until Booking implements its S6 occupancy provider. All Scheduling `/api/v1/**` requests, including `OPTIONS`, require bearer authentication and Identity authorization; Identity is authoritative for actor/permissions and unauthenticated, inactive, denied, or unavailable cases fail closed. The service routes are not configured on the API Gateway in Stage 5; Gateway publication is Stage 11. Direct authorized service access is not equivalent to public routing and production network/ingress policy must keep the service and its OpenAPI surfaces private. Room retains maintenance, and Booking owns confirmed reservation occupancy and double-booking prevention in S6.

## Booking reservation lifecycle

Booking's protected reservation lifecycle routes are `POST /api/v1/reservations/{reservationId}/check-in` and `POST /api/v1/reservations/{reservationId}/complete`. Identity authorizes the active organizer only within current department/building scope, a lifecycle manager within assigned building scope, or a global system administrator. Booking locks the persisted reservation and allows only `CONFIRMED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`; `CANCELLED` and `RELEASED` are terminal. Actor identity and timestamps are server-derived. The explicitly approved ERD field `reservation.completed_at` is required exactly when status is `COMPLETED`, enforced in Flyway V3. Occupancy remains a half-open interval with release buffer and database exclusion constraints. No automatic missed-check-in or overdue release runs, and no Gateway route is configured.

## Next stage

Stages 3, 4, and 5 are accepted, and the Stage 6 checklist is approved in [Backend Documentation](../Documentation.md#stage-6-requirement-checklist-approved). S6-01 through S6-11 are complete and verified. Booking exposes protected request create/list/get, draft-submit, decision, `POST /api/v1/bookings/direct`, check-in, completion, meeting, participant, cancellation, extension, and share-link management APIs. Approval/rejection decisions revalidate current Identity authorization and Organization, Room, and Scheduling owner data, plus Booking-owned occupancy. Approval atomically persists the decision, confirmed reservation, private meeting, and requester organizer participant; rejection persists no reservation or meeting. Direct booking applies the active Room rule consistently to Admin, Super Admin, and eligible Secretary initiators: `approvalRequired=true` creates only a `PENDING_APPROVAL` request and no reservation/meeting until a different authorized reviewer decides it; otherwise confirmation atomically creates the reservation, every validated occurrence, and its meeting. The user approved a Booking-owned `reservation_occurrence` table: one series-level reservation has one child per evaluated local date, each with a buffered half-open range protected by a PostgreSQL GiST exclusion constraint. Scheduling returns actual local occurrence intervals evaluated from the stored RRULE and IANA timezone with the existing one-year/365-occurrence and DST bounds. Booking validates every occurrence against scheduling rules, the effective Room policy, Room maintenance, and Booking occupancy before writing. If any occurrence requires approval, the series waits for the existing decision flow; the greatest applicable release buffer is applied to every interval. Scheduling and maintenance conflicts fail before writes; Booking occupancy conflicts report local dates and roll back every write. Exclusion violations and PostgreSQL deadlocks at overlapping writes map to `ROOM_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`, with the exclusion constraint remaining the final barrier. Lifecycle routes permit only protected `CONFIRMED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, with timestamps generated by the server and `completed_at` constrained by Flyway V3 iff status is `COMPLETED`. Identity checks current organizer/building/department or scoped manager authority. Meeting reads and participant changes use server-derived Booking facts and Identity's active account/scope: PRIVATE is limited to the organizer or in-scope reviewer, INTERNAL additionally allows active same-building users, and PUBLIC visibility does not make protected meeting APIs anonymous. Internal participant identity/display data is validated by Identity; external contact data remains Booking-owned; participant writes are organizer/reviewer-only and serialized on the meeting row. Cancellation and extension operations are exposed only on protected Booking service routes (no Gateway route). Cancellation checks the effective Room deadline, persists actor/reason/deadline/override history, and transactionally releases occurrence occupancy without deleting history. Extension operations retain request/decision history, enforce scoped non-self decisions, revalidate Scheduling/Room constraints and maintenance, recompute the release buffer, and update reservation plus occurrence occupancy under existing locks and exclusion constraints. The existing reservation start is not re-screened against new-booking advance windows, and recurring reservations cannot be extended. S6-11 adds protected share-link creation/revocation and a single unauthenticated token read returning only title, start/end instants, stored IANA timezone, and trusted organizer display name. Raw 256-bit tokens are returned only on creation; only SHA-256 hashes, expiry, revocation, and view counts are persisted. Its only ERD change is the user-approved `MEETING_SHARE_LINK.timezone VARCHAR(64) NOT NULL`; Flyway V5 fails rather than invent legacy timezones. Public reads have a bounded process-local rate limit, and no Booking route is Gateway-routed. There is no automatic overdue release. The user selected all-or-nothing recurring-series conflict handling, with no partial persistence when an occurrence fails. S6-11 verification passed 33 focused Booking tests, 53 Identity tests, 6 common-web tests, and 295 tests across the full 11-module backend reactor, all with zero failures/errors/skips.

### S6-08 meeting visibility and participant ownership

Booking owns its Meeting and MeetingParticipant records and the one-to-one Reservation/Meeting constraint. For each read or mutation, Booking loads the persisted reservation and booking request, verifies requester/organizer, room, building, and department facts are consistent, and forwards the caller bearer token to Identity. Identity rechecks the current active account and scope: PRIVATE allows the organizer or a scoped Booking reviewer; INTERNAL adds active users currently assigned to the meeting building. The `PUBLIC` enum value does not make protected meeting endpoints public; a separate share-link endpoint returns only its explicit public-card fields. Participant additions cannot set actor, organizer role, or invite status. Internal account names come from Identity and must be active in the same building; external participant email/name is validated and an external participant is accepted only if the persisted booking request's Room-policy-validated `externalGuests` flag is true. Meeting-row locking serializes additions and removals to enforce duplicate checks without changing the approved ERD. Meeting/participant routes are not Gateway-routed.
